📚 Work, Power & Energy Study Guide

Review these concepts before playing!

1. Work

Work Formula: W = F × d × cos(θ)
Simpler form: W = F × d (when force and displacement are in same direction)

Definition: Work is the energy transferred when a force moves an object through a distance.

  • Unit: Joule (J) = Newton-meter (N·m)
  • F = Force applied (in Newtons)
  • d = Displacement (in meters)
  • θ = Angle between force and displacement
  • Work is ONLY done when force causes displacement in its direction
  • If force is perpendicular to motion, NO work is done (cos(90°) = 0)

2. Power

Power Formula: P = W ÷ t
Alternative: P = F × v (when force and velocity are in same direction)

Definition: Power is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred.

  • Unit: Watt (W) = Joule per second (J/s)
  • W = Work done (in Joules)
  • t = Time (in seconds)
  • v = Velocity (in m/s)
  • More power = work done faster

3. Kinetic Energy

Kinetic Energy Formula: KE = ½ × m × v²

Definition: Energy of motion. An object in motion has kinetic energy.

  • Unit: Joule (J)
  • m = Mass (in kilograms)
  • v = Velocity (in m/s)
  • KE is proportional to v² → doubling speed quadruples KE!

Work-Energy Theorem

Wnet = ΔKE = KEfinal − KEinitial

The net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy.

4. Potential Energy

Gravitational Potential Energy

PE = m × g × h

Definition: Energy stored due to an object's position above a reference point.

  • m = Mass (in kilograms)
  • g = Gravitational acceleration (≈ 10 m/s² or 9.8 m/s²)
  • h = Height above reference point (in meters)
  • Reference point (h = 0) can be chosen anywhere
  • Only changes in PE matter, not absolute values

Elastic Potential Energy (Springs)

PEelastic = ½ × k × x²
  • k = Spring constant (in N/m)
  • x = Displacement from equilibrium (in meters)
  • Energy stored in stretched or compressed springs

5. Conservation of Energy

Einitial = Efinal
PEinitial + KEinitial = PEfinal + KEfinal

Law: Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another.

  • In a closed system with no friction, total mechanical energy stays constant
  • As object falls: PE decreases, KE increases (PE → KE)
  • As object rises: KE decreases, PE increases (KE → PE)
  • Used to solve problems with pendulums, roller coasters, falling objects

Key Problem-Solving Tips

  • ✓ Always identify known and unknown values first
  • ✓ Choose appropriate formula based on what's given
  • ✓ Check units: convert if necessary (kg, m, s, J)
  • ✓ For energy conservation: Einitial = Efinal
  • ✓ Draw diagrams to visualize the problem
  • ✓ Remember: cos(0°) = 1, cos(90°) = 0, cos(60°) = 0.5
